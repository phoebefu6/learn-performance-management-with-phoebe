# Official course map - learn-performance-management-with-phoebe

**Course:** Performance management as the craft of growing people - and, when it comes to it, exiting them well. Ramp, coaching, growth, ladders, promotion, underperformance, improvement plans, and dignified exits.
**Scope:** The manager's half of performance: what happens in the first 90 days, how to coach rather than fix, how to build a growth plan someone actually uses, how a career ladder translates into observable behaviour, how to diagnose underperformance before intervening, how to run an improvement plan that is a genuine chance rather than paperwork, how to hold the conversations everyone avoids, and how to end an employment relationship with dignity and a defensible process.
**Arc:** Topic-session, two-track, running company **Cartwheel** - deliberately the SAME fictional retailer as the sibling `learn-okr-with-phoebe`, so the two courses describe one company's measurement system and one company's people system.
**Audience:** CHRO, heads of function and exec -> the **Leader / Design track (a1-a6)**; team leads and middle managers -> the **Manager / Run track (b1-b10)**. Individual employees can read either as knowledge-sharing.
**Bucket:** `lead` (Leadership & Management). Difficulty 3. Palette: pine `#14532D` + terracotta `#C2410C` on charcoal.
**Build mode:** built direct from verified public sources (below). Course-taking loop paused.

## The course thesis (carry through every session)

1. **Most underperformance is diagnosed wrong.** Will, skill, fit, and context need four different interventions, and using the wrong one wastes months and a person.
2. **The plan is not the intervention - the teaching is.** An improvement plan with no instruction inside it is a countdown.
3. **No plan should ever be the first time someone hears it.** If the conversation is a surprise, the manager already failed months earlier.
4. **You cannot improvement-plan your way out of a problem that was never the person.** Fix the role, the resourcing, or the manager.
5. **Recovery and defensibility are two different things**, moved by different levers. Coaching and honest early warning buy recovery; documented evidence and consistency buy defensibility. A plan needs both.
6. **The manager is the system.** Google's Project Oxygen put "is a good coach" first among manager behaviours; no framework compensates for a manager who will not coach.
7. **Scope, not tenure.** Ladders describe observable scope of impact. Time served is not a level.
8. **Exits teach the people who stay.** How someone leaves is read by everyone remaining as a statement of what the organisation actually values.

## The PIP Lab (`assets/pip-live.js`) - verified canon

Two meters: **genuine recovery chance** and **procedural defensibility**. Six build-it-properly levers, two anti-levers. Recovery is a share of what the CAUSE can achieve at best (each case has a ceiling); defensibility is about process, so its span is the same for every case.

Verified ladder, skill-gap case (recovery% / defensibility%). **Read live in the browser, not computed on paper** - two intermediate rungs round differently between a paper model and the page, so these are the page's own numbers:

| State | Recovery | Defensibility |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing in place | 22% | 8% |
| + written criteria the person can meet | 37% | 24% |
| + evidence from before the plan | 42% | 43% |
| + weekly coaching with named support | 61% | 51% |
| + the honest early warning | 75% | 63% |
| + a realistic timeline | 87% | 75% |
| + consistency with similar cases | **88%** | **92%** |
| all six, then "shorten it to two weeks" | 32% | 36% |
| all six, then "write it after deciding to terminate" | 5% | 13% |

The teaching beats: **coaching and the honest early warning move recovery; evidence and the consistency check move defensibility** - a plan needs both, and they are not the same work. The **context-mismatch case tops out at 31% recovery even with all six levers on and defensibility at 92%** - a clean, defensible process aimed at the wrong cause. That contrast is the point of the whole session.

Honesty rail carried on the page and in the lab: a deterministic teaching model on a fictional company, not a prediction engine and not legal advice.

## Hard rails / honesty

- **Cartwheel is a fictional teaching company.** Its people and cases are invented.
- **Not legal advice.** Acas, TAFEP and US at-will are summarised for awareness, dated, and differ by jurisdiction. Employment law is local and fact-specific; consult counsel before acting on any real case.
- **No fabricated statistics.** The widely-circulated claim that "roughly 75% of employees put on a PIP leave anyway" traces to vendor blogs rather than research and is deliberately NOT stated as fact anywhere in this course. The defensible version is used instead: plans are frequently written as formality before a predetermined termination, and that is precisely what is now being litigated.
- **The keeper test is taught with its critique**, not as best practice: it is exposed to manager bias, including the tendency to rate people who resemble us more highly.
- **Diagnose before intervening.** The course refuses to treat a plan as a default response to underperformance.
- **Dignity is not softness.** A clean process and a humane delivery are the same skill, not a trade-off.

## Not covered by design (say so honestly)

- Goal-setting, OKR writing, metric design, rater-bias mechanics and calibration - owned by `learn-okr-with-phoebe`.
- Compensation and pay-band design (a separate discipline; we only cover the pay *conversation*).
- Recruitment and interviewing craft beyond the hiring bar's effect on later performance.
- HRIS and performance-tool tutorials - principles, not products.
- Jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction employment law beyond UK, Singapore and US-at-will illustrations.
- Clinical or medical capability questions, disability accommodation specifics, and grievance or harassment investigations - all need specialist and legal input.
